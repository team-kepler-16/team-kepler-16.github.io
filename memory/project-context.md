# Project Context

- **Goal:** Develop a Mars-only solution for the NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026 challenge [Abandoned but Not Forgotten: Storytelling About NASA's Discarded Equipment on the Moon and Mars](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/abandoned-but-not-forgotten-storytelling-about-nasas-discarded-equipment-on-the-moon-and-mars/).
- **Current stack:** HTML5, CSS3, JavaScript, and jQuery 4.0.0 loaded from the official CDN. Additional technologies may be introduced later.
- **JavaScript preference:** Use jQuery for DOM work when convenient; use native JavaScript when clearer.
- **Code organization:** Keep `js/mars.js` as the Three.js scene entry point, with globe interaction in `js/mars/globe-controls.js` and marker rendering in `js/mars/marker-field.js`. The scene passes its point button collection to the controls, and the marker field groups each marker's geometry and visual state in one record. Keep shared site controls and translations in `js/script.js`.
- **Responsive requirement:** The site must display and remain usable on both desktop and mobile devices.
- **Mars visualization:** Show a slowly auto-rotating 3D Mars sphere with cartoon shading, starting slightly zoomed out, on a full-viewport canvas behind the floating navbar. Frame the globe in the available area below the navbar; zoom may extend beyond the screen edges. Randomly place interactive cyan discs on the globe surface, with surface-conforming soft halos, enlarged click targets, paired radar-style waves, and a red outline, interior, and halo on hover that pauses automatic rotation. Clicking a marker or its list entry opens an in-app point window, centers and highlights that marker, and pauses globe movement until the window closes. Users can rotate the globe by dragging horizontally with the mouse or one finger, around its vertical axis, and zoom with the mouse wheel or a two-finger pinch while no point window is open. It uses Three.js and the NASA/JPL/Caltech cartoon texture in `assets/mars-texture-cartoon.png`, credited to [NASA's Mars 3D resources](https://science.nasa.gov/3d-resources/mars/).
- **Homepage title:** “NASA: 50 Years of Landings on Mars,” translated for the Spanish locale and aligned left of the navbar on desktop. On small screens, center it on one line above the centered navigation controls.
- **Credits:** Keep the Mars texture attribution and source link on `about.html`.
- **Design and navigation:** Use a modern Mars-exploration visual language with layered space backgrounds, mineral/rust tones, editorial typography, atmospheric lighting, and cyan signal accents that match the interactive markers. Show the 18 point labels in a right-side list, localized with the rest of the interface. On desktop, let the panel expand to the available height with internal scrolling as needed; keep Mars centered in the viewport. Preserve the current panel and globe framing on mobile. Each list item maps to its globe marker; hover or keyboard focus rotates the globe in the same negative direction around its vertical axis, aligning the marker with the horizontal centerline while preserving its latitude and highlighting it red. Clicking a marker or list item opens its numbered point window with a split reveal from the center, fills nearly all space below the header over a blurred background, and holds the globe still until close. Keep the floating icon controls for sound mute/unmute and English/Spanish locale, show the destination language flag on the locale control, and keep the About link. Ensure the design remains readable and usable on desktop and mobile.
- **Documentation language:** English.
